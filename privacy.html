Privacy policy

esoji for Mac and iPhone · Updated November 22, 2026

esoji works on your photos on your own device. It has no account, no servers of its own, no analytics and no tracking. Nobody, including its developer, receives your photos or anything esoji works out from them.

What esoji reads

With your permission, esoji reads your Photos library to show your photos and build an index of them: what's in each photo, faces it groups together, duplicates and similar shots, and what you've chosen to keep. The index and everything esoji learns from your choices are stored only in esoji's own storage on that device.

What leaves your device

  • The model download. The first time, esoji downloads its search model (about 470 MB on iPhone, about 3 GB on Mac) from Hugging Face (huggingface.co). This is a plain file download; it sends nothing about you or your photos. Hugging Face, like any website, sees the request's IP address.
  • Update check (Mac only). At most once a day, the Mac app asks GitHub for its latest public release, with an esoji/<version> User-Agent and nothing about you or your photos. You can turn it off in Settings → Updates.
  • Claude Code or Codex (Mac only, optional, off by default). If you choose one of them for the chat, the text of your requests goes to Anthropic or OpenAI after you agree, with photos replaced by handles and people by stand-in names. Photos themselves are never sent. What those companies keep is set by your account with them.

Search understanding with Apple Intelligence runs on Apple's on-device model. esoji for iPhone sends nothing else anywhere: no photos, no index, no usage data, no crash reports.

What esoji changes

esoji only changes your library when you ask: it can favorite photos and add them to albums, and it hides or deletes only the photos you confirm, after iOS or macOS asks you too. Deleted photos stay in Photos' Recently Deleted for 30 days.

Data we collect

None. esoji has no sign-in and doesn't collect personal information, usage data, identifiers or diagnostics, and it doesn't share or sell anything. If you join a TestFlight beta, Apple shares with the developer what TestFlight provides (such as your name or email if you're invited by email, and any feedback or crash reports you choose to send); that's covered by Apple's privacy policy.

Deleting your data

Delete the app and everything esoji stored is deleted with it. On Mac, Settings → Privacy also deletes your chats or all of esoji's data, and hiding a photo in esoji deletes everything it worked out from that photo.

Children

esoji isn't directed at children and collects no data from anyone.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date. Questions: open an issue on GitHub.
